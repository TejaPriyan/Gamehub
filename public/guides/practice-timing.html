Practice gravity flips and grid turns

Two exercises that make arcade movement more deliberate.

By Teja Priyan · Updated 27 October 2026

Grav-Runner: look past the next obstacle

In Grav-Runner, Space, the up arrow or the touch action flips gravity. Treat the action as a change of destination rather than a jump with a fixed landing point. Look at the next safe section on the opposite surface before switching. Rapidly pressing the control without a plan can turn a recoverable position into another collision.

For an initial practice run, ignore optional pickups and focus on reaching safe space. Watch one complete transition and learn how long it takes. Then try collecting an item only when the route after it is also clear. A short successful segment teaches more than repeating an uncontrolled opening.

Neon Lightcycle: leave an escape route

The grid game turns in right angles and leaves a trail. Arrows or WASD control direction; touch navigation is available in the game. Watch the space you will need after the next turn. A corner that appears safe now can trap you against a wall or your own trail a moment later.

Practice a broad route that preserves empty space before attempting tight turns or speed changes. Plan two turns ahead: where you will turn now and which exit remains afterward. If an input appears to be ignored, check whether the requested direction would immediately reverse your current path.

Measure one improvement

Set a small practice goal, such as making the opening gravity transition consistently or completing a clean loop on the grid. Compare runs under similar conditions rather than changing the game, device and input method at once. Stop and reset when you lose track of the controls. These games are for recreation: local records do not carry cash value, and no score is a prediction of performance in another game.
